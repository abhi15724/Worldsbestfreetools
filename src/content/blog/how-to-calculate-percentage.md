**Quick answer:** to find a percentage of a number, multiply by the percentage and divide by 100. So **20% of 500 = 500 × 20 ÷ 100 = 100**. Percent literally means "per hundred", which is why the 100 appears in every formula. Use our free [Percentage Calculator](/percentage-calculator/) to check any value instantly, and read on for the other calculations you are likely to need.

## What a percentage is

A percentage expresses a part of a whole as a fraction of 100. 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 25/100, which equals 0.25 as a decimal and ¼ as a fraction. Because 100 is an easy common base, percentages let you compare quantities of different sizes: 18 out of 24 and 45 out of 60 are both 75%.

You can switch between forms easily. To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by 100 (35% = 0.35). To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100 (0.08 = 8%).

## The five percentage calculations you will use most

### 1. Percentage of a number

**Result = Number × Percentage ÷ 100**

Examples:
- 15% of 800 = 800 × 15 ÷ 100 = **120**
- 7.5% of 2,000 = 2,000 × 7.5 ÷ 100 = **150**
- 20% of 500 = **100**

This is the calculation behind a tip, a commission, a tax amount or a share of a budget.

### 2. What percent one number is of another

**Percentage = Part ÷ Whole × 100**

Examples:
- You scored 45 out of 60: 45 ÷ 60 × 100 = **75%**.
- You spent ₹3,000 of a ₹12,000 budget: 3,000 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = **25%**.

### 3. Percentage increase

**Increase % = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100**

A price rises from ₹80 to ₹100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = **25%** increase. Notice that you divide by the **old** value, not the new one.

### 4. Percentage decrease

**Decrease % = (Old − New) ÷ Old × 100**

A phone price drops from ₹20,000 to ₹17,000: (20,000 − 17,000) ÷ 20,000 × 100 = **15%** decrease.

### 5. Adding or subtracting a percentage

To add a percentage, multiply by (1 + percentage ÷ 100). To subtract, multiply by (1 − percentage ÷ 100).

- ₹1,200 plus 10% = 1,200 × 1.10 = **₹1,320**
- ₹2,500 minus 20% = 2,500 × 0.80 = **₹2,000**

## Everyday examples

**Discounts.** A jacket costs ₹3,200 with a 35% discount. The discount is 3,200 × 35 ÷ 100 = ₹1,120, so you pay ₹2,080. Or multiply once: 3,200 × 0.65 = ₹2,080.

**Tips.** A restaurant bill is ₹1,450 and you want to leave 10%: 1,450 × 10 ÷ 100 = ₹145.

**Exam marks.** If a test has 80 marks and you got 62, your score is 62 ÷ 80 × 100 = 77.5%.

**Salary hikes.** A 12% raise on ₹40,000 adds ₹4,800 for a new salary of ₹44,800.

**Interest.** Simple yearly interest of 7% on ₹50,000 is 50,000 × 7 ÷ 100 = ₹3,500. For interest that compounds, see our guide to [compound interest](/blog/compound-interest-explained/).

## Percentage points versus percent

A frequent source of confusion: if an interest rate moves from 6% to 8%, it has risen by **2 percentage points**, but it has increased by **33.3%** in relative terms ((8 − 6) ÷ 6 × 100). News reports and loan quotes can mean either, so check which one is meant before comparing figures.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Using the wrong base.** For increase and decrease, always divide by the original value.
2. **Assuming up and down cancel out.** A 50% rise followed by a 50% fall leaves you 25% below where you started (100 → 150 → 75), because the fall applies to the larger number. To undo a 25% rise you need a 20% fall.
3. **Adding successive discounts.** Two discounts of 20% and 10% do not equal 30%. Take 20% off first (× 0.80), then 10% off the result (× 0.90): the combined multiplier is 0.72, a 28% total discount.
4. **Confusing percent and percentage points**, as explained above.
5. **Forgetting to convert.** Typing 8 instead of 0.08 in a spreadsheet formula gives a result 100 times too large. Use the % symbol in a spreadsheet or divide by 100.

## A mental-maths shortcut

To find 10% of any number, move the decimal point one place left: 10% of 460 is 46. From that you can build others: 5% is half of 10% (23), 20% is double (92), and 15% is 10% plus 5% (69). For 1%, move two places left. These tricks make quick estimates easy when you are shopping or splitting a bill.

## Key takeaways

- Percentage of a number = number × percentage ÷ 100.
- Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100, always relative to the old value.
- Successive percentages multiply; they do not simply add.
- Percentage points and percent are different things.
