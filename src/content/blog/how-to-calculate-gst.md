**Quick answer:** to **add GST**, multiply the price by the rate and add it on: ₹10,000 + 18% GST = ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 = **₹11,800**. To **remove GST** from a price that already includes it, divide by 1 plus the rate: ₹11,800 ÷ 1.18 = **₹10,000**, so the GST inside was ₹1,800. ## What GST is

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is the indirect tax charged on most supplies of goods and services in India. Sellers add it to the price, collect it from customers and pass it to the government. Registered businesses can usually claim credit for GST they paid on their own purchases (called input tax credit), so the tax is meant to fall on the final consumer.

## Current GST rates in India

On 22 September 2025, following the 56th GST Council meeting, India moved to a simpler rate structure. The old 12% and 28% slabs were largely removed. Today the main rates are:

| Rate | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 0% | Essentials such as many fresh foods and grains |
| 5% | Everyday goods and some services |
| 18% | Standard rate for most goods and services |
| 40% | A narrow set of luxury and demerit goods |

Some supplies keep special rates outside this grid, such as gold at 3%, and certain items are exempt. Because the rate depends on the product's classification (HSN code for goods, SAC for services), always confirm the rate on the official GST Council or CBIC notification or with your accountant. Invoices for supplies made before 22 September 2025 may still be governed by the old rates. You can read the Council's announcement in its [official press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau_0.pdf).

## How to add GST to a price

**GST amount = Price × Rate ÷ 100**
**Total price = Price + GST amount**

Example: a service costs ₹10,000 before tax and the rate is 18%.

- GST = 10,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹1,800
- Total = 10,000 + 1,800 = **₹11,800**

At 5% the same service would carry ₹500 of GST and cost ₹10,500. At 40% it would carry ₹4,000.

## How to remove GST from an inclusive price

Customers often see one final price and want to know how much of it is tax. This is called a reverse GST calculation.

**Pre-tax price = Inclusive price ÷ (1 + Rate ÷ 100)**
**GST amount = Inclusive price − Pre-tax price**

Example: you paid ₹11,800 and the rate is 18%.

- Pre-tax price = 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = **₹10,000**
- GST = 11,800 − 10,000 = **₹1,800**

### A very common mistake

Many people take 18% of ₹11,800 (which is ₹2,124) and subtract it, getting ₹9,676. That is wrong because the 18% was charged on the pre-tax price, not on the total. The GST in an inclusive price is always smaller than the rate applied to the inclusive price. Use the division method above, or choose "Remove GST" in the calculator.

## CGST, SGST and IGST

How the tax is split depends on where the buyer and seller are.

- **Within one state (intra-state):** the rate is split equally into **CGST** (Central GST) and **SGST** (State GST). At 18% that is 9% CGST + 9% SGST. On ₹10,000 you would see ₹900 of each.
- **Between states (inter-state):** a single **IGST** equal to the full rate applies. At 18% that is 18% IGST, or ₹1,800 on ₹10,000.
- **Union territories without a legislature** charge UTGST in place of SGST.

The total tax is the same either way. Only the invoice breakdown changes, and that matters for filing returns and for claiming input credit.

## Using GST in business

- **Invoicing:** show the taxable value, the rate and the tax split on each invoice, as the GST rules require for registered taxpayers.
- **Pricing:** decide whether your displayed price includes GST. If it does, use reverse calculation to find your actual revenue before tax.
- **Input tax credit:** registered businesses can usually offset GST paid on purchases against GST collected on sales, subject to conditions. Keep invoices and match them with your returns.
- **Rounding:** compute tax per invoice line or per invoice as your accounting software and the law allow, and round consistently. Tiny differences between a calculator and your invoice are usually rounding.

## Key takeaways

- Add GST by multiplying; remove it by dividing by (1 + rate).
- India's main GST rates are now 5% and 18%, with 40% for a narrow set of items and 0% for essentials.
- Intra-state sales split the tax into CGST and SGST; inter-state sales charge IGST.
- Always confirm the rate for your specific product or service from official sources.
