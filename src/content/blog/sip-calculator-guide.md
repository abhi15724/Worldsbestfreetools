**Quick answer:** a SIP (systematic investment plan) means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly. If you invest ₹5,000 every month for 10 years and your investments grow at an assumed 12% a year, the pot would be about **₹11.6 lakh**, of which ₹6 lakh is your own money and about ₹5.6 lakh is growth. Try your own numbers in our free [SIP Calculator](/sip-calculator/). The rest of this guide shows how the maths works and where the estimate can mislead you.

## What a SIP is, and what it is not

A SIP is a way of investing, not an investment in itself. You commit to putting in a set sum each month, most often into mutual funds, though the same idea works for stocks or index funds. Because you buy regularly, you automatically buy more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. This habit, often called rupee-cost averaging, removes the pressure to time the market and builds discipline.

A SIP does not guarantee a return. The 12% in the examples below is an assumption you type into a calculator, not a promise. Actual results depend on the funds you choose, how long you stay invested and what markets do.

## The SIP future value formula

For a monthly SIP, the future value is:

**FV = M × (((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i) × (1 + i)**

- **M** is the amount invested each month.
- **i** is the monthly rate of return, which is the annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100.
- **n** is the total number of monthly instalments.

The final (1 + i) multiplier assumes each instalment is invested at the **start** of the month, which is how our calculator works. Some calculators assume payment at the end of the month and show a slightly smaller result, so two tools can differ by a small amount even with the same inputs.

## Worked example: ₹5,000 a month at 12% for 10 years

1. M = 5,000
2. i = 12 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.01
3. n = 10 × 12 = 120
4. (1.01)¹²⁰ ≈ 3.3004
5. FV = 5,000 × ((3.3004 − 1) ÷ 0.01) × 1.01 ≈ **₹11,61,695**

You invested 5,000 × 120 = ₹6,00,000. The estimated gain is about **₹5,61,695**, so close to half of the final amount came from growth rather than your deposits.

## Time matters more than the amount

The same ₹5,000 a month, at the same assumed 12%, gives very different results depending on how long you stay invested:

| Duration | You invest | Estimated value | Estimated gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 years | ₹3,00,000 | ₹4,12,432 | ₹1,12,432 |
| 10 years | ₹6,00,000 | ₹11,61,695 | ₹5,61,695 |
| 15 years | ₹9,00,000 | ₹25,22,880 | ₹16,22,880 |
| 20 years | ₹12,00,000 | ₹49,95,740 | ₹37,95,740 |
| 25 years | ₹15,00,000 | ₹94,88,175 | ₹79,88,175 |

Doubling the time from 10 to 20 years does not double the result; it more than quadruples it, because growth earns growth. Going from 20 to 25 years adds ₹45 lakh of value for only ₹3 lakh of extra deposits. This compounding effect is the strongest argument for starting early, even with a small amount.

## The return assumption is the biggest uncertainty

Changing the expected return changes the outcome sharply. For ₹5,000 a month over 10 years, an assumed 10% gives about ₹10.3 lakh while 12% gives about ₹11.6 lakh. Raising the monthly amount to ₹10,000 at 12% doubles the result to about ₹23.2 lakh. When you plan with a calculator, try a conservative rate as well as an optimistic one. If the conservative figure still meets your goal, your plan is more robust.

Remember too that returns arrive unevenly. A fund averaging 12% over 10 years may have had years of large gains and years of losses. Equity investments can fall sharply in the short term, so a SIP aimed at a goal in under about five years usually calls for lower-risk choices.

## Step-up SIP: raise your investment as your income grows

Most people earn more over time, so keeping the same SIP for 20 years undersells your capacity. A step-up SIP increases the instalment each year by a set percentage. Starting at ₹5,000 with a 10% yearly step-up, you would pay ₹5,500 a month in year two, ₹6,050 in year three and so on. Our [Step-Up SIP Calculator](/step-up-sip-calculator/) models this, and the difference to a flat SIP over 15 to 20 years is large.

## Inflation, taxes and costs

A big rupee number in 20 years is not the same as big purchasing power. If inflation averages 6%, ₹50 lakh in 20 years buys roughly what ₹15.6 lakh buys today. Also remember that fund expense ratios reduce returns, and gains may be taxed when you redeem. Capital gains tax rules for mutual funds change from time to time, so check the current rules or ask a tax professional. A calculator's output is a **before-tax, before-inflation** estimate.

## How to use a SIP calculator well

1. Enter the amount you can invest every month without stress.
2. Test three return rates: conservative, moderate and optimistic.
3. Compare results at 5, 10 and 20 years.
4. Work backwards from your goal: if you need ₹50 lakh in 15 years, try amounts until the result matches, then ask if that amount is realistic.
5. Revisit the plan once a year and raise the amount when your income rises.

## Key takeaways

- A SIP is a habit of investing regularly, and the calculator shows an estimate, not a promise.
- Duration and consistency drive results more than the exact return you assume.
- Test conservative scenarios, and account for inflation, costs and tax.
- A step-up SIP lets your investing keep pace with your income.
