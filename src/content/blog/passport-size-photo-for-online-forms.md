## Why photo size is confusing

Photo requirements mix three different kinds of measurement:

- **Physical size** in millimetres, centimetres or inches, which matters for printing.
- **Pixel size**, such as 413 × 531, which matters for online forms.
- **File size** in KB or MB, which is the weight of the image file.

A form can ask for any of these, and sometimes all three. A photo can be the right physical size and still be rejected because the file is too large, or the pixels are wrong.

## Common passport photo sizes in mm, inches and pixels

The pixel size depends on the resolution. At 300 dpi (a common print setting):

| Photo size | Inches | Pixels at 300 dpi |
|---|---|---|
| 35 × 45 mm (3.5 × 4.5 cm) | 1.38 × 1.77 | 413 × 531 |
| 40 × 60 mm (4 × 6 cm) | 1.57 × 2.36 | 472 × 709 |
| 2 × 2 inches (US passport) | 2 × 2 | 600 × 600 |

You can work out any size yourself: **pixels = millimetres ÷ 25.4 × dpi**. For 35 mm at 300 dpi, that is 35 ÷ 25.4 × 300 = about 413 pixels. Our [Pixel to CM Converter](/pixel-to-cm-converter/) does this for you.

Requirements differ by country and by form, so always follow the instructions of the office or portal you are applying to. The sizes above are common examples, not a rule for every application.

## How to place your head correctly

Many offices check how large your face is within the photo and how much space is above your head. As a general guide:

- For **35 × 45 mm** photos, the head from chin to the top of the head is commonly about **32 to 36 mm**.
- For **US 2 × 2 inch** photos, the head should measure **1 to 1 3/8 inches** from chin to the top of the head.

## How to keep the file under 100 KB or 50 KB

File size depends on pixel size, detail and how the image is compressed. To get a small file without a muddy face:

1. **Start with the right dimensions.** A 413 × 531 pixel photo has far fewer pixels than a 4000-pixel phone photo, so it is much smaller from the start.
2. **Use a plain background.** Flat areas compress well, while patterns and clutter add size.
3. **Use even light.** Shadows and noise make the file larger and the picture worse.

Do not compress the same file repeatedly, since each pass loses detail. Always keep your original photo.

## Tips for taking a good photo

- **Lighting:** face a window or use soft light from the front. Avoid shadows on your face and behind you.
- **Background:** a plain white or light wall with no patterns, doors or furniture.
- **Camera position:** hold the camera at eye level, about an arm's length away, so the face is not distorted.
- **Expression:** neutral, eyes open and looking at the camera, mouth closed.
- **Clothing:** wear something that stands out from the background, and avoid white on a white wall.
- **Glasses and accessories:** follow your form's rules. Glare on glasses is a common reason for rejection.
- **No filters:** do not use beauty filters, heavy retouching or background blur.
