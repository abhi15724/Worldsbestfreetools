## What a QR code is

A QR (Quick Response) code is a square grid of black and white modules that stores data such as a web address, some text or a payment ID. A phone camera reads the pattern and the phone then acts on the data: opening a link, offering to join a WiFi network or starting a payment. The three large squares in the corners help the camera find and orient the code, and extra redundancy built into the pattern lets it be read even if part is damaged or dirty.

The codes our generators create are **static**. The data is written directly into the pattern, so the code works forever and does not depend on any website or service. The trade-off is that the content is fixed once printed. If you need to change the destination later or track scans, you would need a "dynamic" code from a service that redirects through its own server.

## Design and printing tips

- **Keep strong contrast.** Dark code on a light background scans best. Avoid light-on-dark inverted codes, since some scanners cannot read them, and avoid low-contrast colours.
- **Leave a quiet zone.** The QR standard recommends a blank border of about four modules (the small squares) around the code. Our PNG includes a smaller built-in margin, so keep extra white space around it when you print, and do not put text or borders right against the edge.
- **Size it for the distance.** At least about 2 cm per side for something held in the hand, and bigger for posters, with roughly a 10:1 distance-to-width ratio as a guide.
- **Do not stretch or distort it.** Keep the code square and print it sharp. A 512-pixel PNG scales well for most flyers; for large posters, use a vector tool or ask your printer for a larger export.
- **Print on a flat, matte surface.** Glossy reflections, curved bottles and creased paper make scanning unreliable.
- **Add a short call to action.** "Scan to view the menu" tells people what to expect and increases scans.

## Test before you print

Before sending anything to a printer, check the code:

1. Scan it with at least two phones, ideally one iPhone and one Android, using the normal camera app.
2. Check the destination or payment name is correct and the link works on mobile data, not only your WiFi.
3. Print a small test copy at the real size and scan it from the distance people will use.

Errors are cheap to fix on a screen and expensive on 500 printed flyers.

## Staying safe with QR codes

A QR code hides the destination, so scammers sometimes stick fake codes over real ones on parking meters, posters or restaurant tables. When scanning:

- Read the address your phone shows before you open it.
- Be wary of codes in unexpected places or on stickers that look added later.
- Never enter passwords or card details on a page reached from a code you cannot verify.
- For payments, confirm the payee name shown in your payment app before approving.

If you display a QR code for your own business, check regularly that no one has covered it with another sticker.
