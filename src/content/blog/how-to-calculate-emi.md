**Quick answer:** an EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is cleared. It is worked out from three numbers: the loan amount, the interest rate and the tenure. For a ₹10,00,000 loan at 9% a year over 10 years, the EMI is about **₹12,668**, and the total interest is about **₹5,20,109**. You can check any combination in seconds with our free [EMI Calculator](/emi-calculator/); the rest of this guide explains what is happening behind the number.

## What an EMI actually contains

Every EMI is a blend of two parts: interest on the money you still owe, and a repayment of some of the principal. The total stays the same each month, but the mix changes. In the early years most of your payment goes towards interest because the outstanding balance is large. As the balance falls, interest shrinks and a bigger share of each EMI reduces the principal.

This is why paying a loan off early saves so much. Extra money you pay in the first years goes straight to principal, which reduces the base that interest is charged on for every remaining month.

## The EMI formula

The standard formula for a reducing-balance loan is:

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)**

- **P** is the principal, the amount you borrow.
- **r** is the monthly interest rate. Divide the annual rate by 12, then by 100. A 9% annual rate becomes 0.0075 a month.
- **n** is the number of monthly payments. A 10-year loan has 120.

If the interest rate were zero, the formula would collapse to P ÷ n, simply the loan split evenly across the months.

## Worked example: ₹10 lakh at 9% for 10 years

1. P = 10,00,000
2. r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075
3. n = 10 × 12 = 120
4. (1 + r)ⁿ = 1.0075¹²⁰ ≈ 2.4514
5. EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.0075 × 2.4514 ÷ (2.4514 − 1) ≈ **₹12,668**

Over 120 months you pay ₹12,668 × 120 ≈ ₹15,20,109 in total. Subtract the original ₹10,00,000 and the interest cost is about **₹5,20,109**, more than half the amount you borrowed.

## How tenure and rate change the cost

Small changes to rate or tenure have a big effect over many years. The table shows a ₹10,00,000 loan under different terms, calculated with the same formula:

| Rate | Tenure | EMI per month | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9% | 5 years | ₹20,758 | ₹2,45,501 |
| 9% | 10 years | ₹12,668 | ₹5,20,109 |
| 9% | 15 years | ₹10,143 | ₹8,25,680 |
| 9% | 20 years | ₹8,997 | ₹11,59,342 |
| 8% | 10 years | ₹12,133 | ₹4,55,931 |
| 10% | 10 years | ₹13,215 | ₹5,85,809 |

Two lessons stand out. First, stretching a loan from 10 to 20 years cuts the EMI by about ₹3,700 a month but more than doubles the interest, from about ₹5.2 lakh to about ₹11.6 lakh. Second, a single percentage point on the rate changes the total interest by roughly ₹65,000 on this loan, which is why negotiating or refinancing to a lower rate is worth the effort.

## Reducing balance versus flat rate

Most banks and housing finance companies use the **reducing balance** method, which the formula above assumes. Some dealers and small lenders quote a **flat rate**, where interest is charged on the full original amount for the entire tenure. A flat rate of 8% is much more expensive than 8% on a reducing balance, because you keep paying interest on money you have already repaid. When comparing offers, ask for the effective annual rate (the reducing-balance equivalent), and use a calculator to compare like with like.

## Ways to lower your EMI or total interest

1. **Make a larger down payment.** Every rupee you do not borrow saves interest for the whole tenure.
2. **Compare rates before you sign.** Even a small difference compounds over years. Check processing fees and prepayment rules too, not just the headline rate.
3. **Choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.** A higher EMI that fits your budget costs far less in total.
4. **Prepay when you have spare cash.** A bonus or maturing deposit used to prepay principal shortens the loan. Our [Loan Prepayment Calculator](/loan-prepayment-calculator/) shows how many months and how much interest an extra monthly payment saves.
5. **Ask about refinancing.** If rates have fallen or your credit profile has improved, a lower rate from your current or a new lender can cut the cost. Factor in any transfer fee.
6. **Keep EMIs within a safe share of income.** Lenders and financial planners often use a rule of thumb that total EMIs should stay below roughly 40–50% of take-home pay, but the right limit depends on your other commitments.

## Things a calculator will not show

A calculator gives a clean estimate. In real life, check for: processing fees, insurance bundled with the loan, floating-rate resets (your EMI or tenure can change when the benchmark rate moves), the date interest starts accruing, and penalties for early closure. Floating-rate loans from banks to individual borrowers generally cannot carry prepayment penalties in India, but fixed-rate and business loans may, so read your agreement.

## Key takeaways

- EMI depends on amount, rate and tenure; the formula is standard and the same for every lender using reducing balance.
- Longer tenures mean lower EMIs but much higher total interest.
- Early payments are mostly interest, so prepaying early saves the most.
- Compare the total cost, not just the monthly figure.
