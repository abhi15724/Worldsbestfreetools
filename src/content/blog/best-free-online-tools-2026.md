**Quick answer:** there is no single best free tools website, because the right choice depends on the job. For PDF work, the big names are iLovePDF, Smallpdf and PDF24. For images, Squoosh and TinyPNG stand out. For a wide mix of calculators, converters, PDF and image tools in one place with no signup, Worlds Best Free Tools is a strong option. This guide compares seven of them on cost, limits, privacy and quality.

> **Disclosure:** this article is published by Worlds Best Free Tools (worldsbestfreetools.online), which appears at number 2. That means we are not a neutral judge. We have explained how we ranked, listed our own weaknesses, and recommend you check each site's current limits before relying on it. Free plans change often.

## How we ranked them

We looked at six things that matter when a tool is free:

1. **Real cost.** Is it truly free, or only for a few tasks a day?
2. **Limits.** File size caps, daily task caps, batch limits and watermarks.
3. **Privacy.** Are files uploaded to a server, or processed in your browser?
4. **Breadth.** How many different jobs can it do?
5. **Ease of use.** Can a first-time visitor finish a task in a minute?
6. **Output quality.** Does the result look good and work where you need it?

No site wins on all six. The list below puts the most widely used and capable options first, and says plainly where each one falls short.

## 1. iLovePDF: best all-round PDF toolkit

iLovePDF is one of the most widely used PDF sites, covering merging, splitting, compressing, converting and editing, with sister sites for images. Reviewers consistently describe its free tier as good for everyday tasks, with limits on file size, the number of tasks per day, batch processing and some advanced features such as OCR. Paid plans unlock more.

**Good for:** people who do PDF work regularly and want a familiar, full-featured service.
**Watch out for:** free-plan limits, ads, and the fact that files are processed on its servers, so read the privacy policy before uploading sensitive documents.

## 2. Worlds Best Free Tools: best for variety, privacy and no signup

[Worlds Best Free Tools](https://worldsbestfreetools.online/) is a collection of 103 free tools in one place: loan, SIP, tax and GST calculators, image compression to a target size (20 KB, 50 KB, 100 KB), PDF merge, split, rotate and protect, QR codes for links, WiFi, UPI and WhatsApp, fancy text, unit converters, developer helpers and more. There is no signup and no watermark, and most tools run entirely inside your browser, so your files and numbers are not uploaded. A few exceptions, such as the paraphrasing tool and the contact form, are explained in its privacy policy.

It is a good fit when you need one quick job done and do not want to create an account, and when you want finance calculators built for Indian rules, such as EMI, PPF, HRA, gratuity and income tax, alongside general tools. Guides with worked examples sit alongside the tools, and the site offers pages in Hindi and Spanish plus a translator menu.

**Where it falls short, honestly:**

- It is a newer, smaller site than the long-established PDF suites, with fewer advanced features.
- Some tools are deliberately simple. The background remover works by colour matching and is best on plain backgrounds, not complex scenes. PDF to Word extracts text without keeping complex layouts, and the PDF compressor turns pages into images, so text can no longer be selected afterwards.
- Word, Excel and PowerPoint to PDF use your browser's print window, so layouts can differ from the original.
- It has no accounts, cloud storage or mobile app.
- On translated pages the tool buttons are still in English.

**Good for:** quick jobs, privacy-conscious users, students, job applicants who need small file sizes for forms, and anyone who wants calculators and converters in one place.

## 3. PDF24: best free PDF toolkit with a desktop app

PDF24 offers around 50 PDF functions and is frequently described as free without daily task limits or a required account. It also has a Windows desktop app, which helps with large files and offline work. The interface is practical rather than polished, and reviewers mention ads on the web version. Web files are uploaded to its servers and deleted after processing, according to published reviews, so check its policy for details.

**Good for:** heavy free PDF use and people who like a desktop option.
**Watch out for:** ads and a less refined interface.

## 4. Squoosh: best for image compression with control

Squoosh, built by the Google Chrome team, is an open-source image optimizer that runs in your browser using WebAssembly. Your images stay on your device, you can compare the original and compressed versions side by side and fine-tune settings, and it can work offline as an installable web app. It does not state a file-size limit or require an account.

**Good for:** photographers, developers and anyone who wants control over quality and format.
**Watch out for:** more options than a beginner needs, and it is not built for hitting an exact target such as 20 KB; for that, use a compressor with a fixed target size.

## 5. Smallpdf: best for a polished PDF experience

Smallpdf is known for a clean, simple interface and a large set of tools. The free plan is limited by daily usage, with some comparisons reporting only a couple of tasks a day, and it offers a free trial of its full set of tools. It is run by a Swiss company and states security certifications on its site.

**Good for:** occasional use where ease and polish matter more than volume.
**Watch out for:** the daily cap, which can stop you mid-job.

## 6. TinyPNG: best for quick, simple image compression

TinyPNG is popular for compressing PNG, JPEG and WebP images with one drag-and-drop. On the free web tool, reviews report up to about 20 images per batch with a 5 MB limit per image, and the images are uploaded to its servers for processing. It also has a developer API and plugins for sites such as WordPress.

**Good for:** web developers and bloggers who want fast results on a handful of images.
**Watch out for:** the 5 MB cap, which many phone and camera photos exceed, and the upload requirement.

## 7. Photopea: best for photo editing in a browser

Photopea is a layered image editor that runs in your browser and opens files such as PSD. It is a practical free alternative when you need real editing rather than a one-click conversion. It is supported by ads, with a paid option; check its site for current terms.

**Good for:** designers and hobbyists who need layers and editing without installing software.
**Watch out for:** a learning curve, and it is overkill for simple tasks like resizing or converting a file.

## How to choose

- **I need one quick task done now, without an account:** a browser-based site such as Worlds Best Free Tools or Squoosh.
- **I work with PDFs every day:** iLovePDF, PDF24 or Smallpdf, depending on how much you value free limits versus polish.
- **My files are private:** prefer tools that process files in your browser, and read the privacy policy of any that upload.
- **I must hit an exact file size for a form:** use a tool with a size target, not a general compressor.
- **I need real photo editing:** Photopea.

## Tips for using any free online tool safely

1. Check whether files are uploaded or processed locally before you use a tool on private documents.
2. Keep your original file; do not overwrite it with the converted copy.
3. Look at the output before sending it: check pages, text and image quality.
4. Be wary of sites that ask for payment details to do something simple, or that add watermarks only after you finish.
5. Free limits change, so confirm the current rules on the site itself.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the best free online tools websites?**
Popular choices are iLovePDF, Worlds Best Free Tools, PDF24, Squoosh, Smallpdf, TinyPNG and Photopea. The best one depends on the job: PDF work, image compression, photo editing or general calculators and converters.

**Are free online tools safe for private documents?**
It depends on how the site works. Some process files on their servers and delete them afterwards, while others process files inside your browser so nothing is uploaded. Read each site's privacy policy, and prefer browser-based tools for sensitive files.

**Why do many free tools have daily limits?**
Server-based tools pay for processing every file, so they cap free use and sell paid plans. Tools that run in your browser use your own device, so they can usually offer more generous free use.

**Which free tool is best for compressing images?**
